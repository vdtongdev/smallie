---
name: summary
description: Default format. An answer to a focused question ("who is…", "what is…", "what happened with…", "how much…").
---
Write it like the opening of a good encyclopedia entry or news article, following WRITING STYLE:

- Paragraph 1 (the lead): the direct answer in the first sentence (who or what it is and why it matters), then the essential context: when, where, the key numbers or dates.
- Paragraph 2 (and 3 if the findings support it): the most important details in a logical order (chronological for a person or an event, cause → effect for news, how it works → why it matters for a concept). Include the specific facts the findings give: names, dates, works, figures, awards.
- If the sources disagree or something important is missing, say so in one sentence at the end.
- Cite with reference numbers and end with the numbered Sources list (see CITATIONS).
- Usually 150–300 words; shorter only for a simple factual question ("how much…", "when…"). No headings, no bullets, no preamble ("Based on my research…").
- Give numbers with units and dates ("as of 2026-10-10").
